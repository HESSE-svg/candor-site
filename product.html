From first prompt to the report a carrier asks for.

Six steps, designed to sit inside how a firm already works, no browser babysitting, no intercepting anyone's traffic, no new habit to enforce.

Tap any step to see it.

01

Confidentiality pre-check See it

Before text goes to an outside AI tool, Candor scans it for client identifying details, names, SSNs, account and policy numbers, dates, addresses, medical terms, and returns a redacted version to paste instead. Screening happens in the browser; the text itself is never stored. Try it below.

Confidentiality pre-check
Draft a demand for [NAME], [SSN], policy [POLICY#], re the [DATE] collision.
NAME 1SSN 1POLICY# 1DATE 1
Copy safe version

The scan runs on your device and reads names in their legal role context, not just against a word list, so it flags the client without redacting the caselaw. You paste the safe version; the original never leaves the machine.

02

AI use log See it

Each use is recorded against the matter: which tool, what task, who ran it, when. The firm keeps an approved tools list, and anything logged outside it is flagged, quiet visibility into what's actually being used.

AI use log entry
MatterRivera, J.
ToolChatGPT on approved list
TaskDrafting
Run byD. Alvarez, paralegal

A tool that is not on the approved list is flagged the moment it is logged, so a partner can see at a glance whether staff are using sanctioned tools, without watching over anyone's shoulder.

03

Verification See it

For research and drafting, Candor pulls out case citations and surfaces hedging language worth a second look, then checks each citation's existence against a public legal database. It's a prompt for the reviewer, not a verdict · Candor never claims a citation is correct.

Citation check
✓ 576 U.S. 644 Obergefell v. Hodges
⚠ 812 F.3d 4471 not found, verify manually
appearslikelyit seems

Only the bare citation is sent out for the existence check, never the client's text. The hedging words are the AI's own signals of uncertainty, surfaced so the reviewer knows where to look hardest.

04

Attorney review & sign off See it

A supervising attorney marks the work reviewed and attests they independently checked the citations and assertions. Entries are append only: a change is a new record referencing the last, so the history stays intact.

Attorney sign off
☑ I have independently verified all citations and factual assertions in this output.
M. Okonkwo, Esq. · 14 Apr 2026, 09:41 EDT

The attestation wording is fixed and deliberate. Because entries are append only, a later correction shows up as its own record, so the trail reads like a ledger, not a document someone could quietly rewrite.

05

Reports See it

A firm wide summary and a renewal questionnaire mapped to the questions carriers ask, generated from the record and exportable to PDF. This is the artifact you hand across the table.

Carrier renewal questionnaire
Export PDF

Same data in, same PDF out, so two people generating the report for the same period get the identical artifact. The renewal version is mapped in advance to the AI questions carriers now ask.

06

Policy management See it

Adopt a written AI use policy; staff acknowledge it with a timestamp and re acknowledge whenever it changes. The acknowledgements live in the same record as everything else.

Policy acknowledgements

When the policy changes, everyone acknowledges again, and the earlier acknowledgements stay on file. You can show not just the current policy but the history of who agreed to what, and when.

What the citation check can and cannot tell you. A match confirms the case exists, not that it stands for what the AI said. No match may mean the citation was invented, or simply that it sits outside the public database. Either way, it points a reviewer to look. It never decides, and the attorney's own review is what carries weight.

It reads only what is on the screen, and only when asked. Nothing leaves the browser except the bare citations it needs to look up. No traffic interception, and no monitoring of your people.
