In plain terms

What it actually is.

A website your firm signs into

Plus a small companion for Chrome or Edge, if you want it, that works inside the AI tools your staff already open. Nothing is installed on a server of yours.

It is not an AI tool itself

Candor does not write anything, answer questions, or do legal work. It sits beside whatever your team already uses and keeps track of what happened.

You do not switch anything

Keep ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, whatever the firm runs today. Candor is not a replacement for any of them.

What you end up with is a report

That is the point of the whole thing. Everything else exists so that report is true when you hand it to someone.

One afternoon at a small firm

Here is what actually happens.

Nothing below is a metaphor. This is the software running.

01

A paralegal needs a demand letter

She has the client's file notes in front of her and she is about to paste them into ChatGPT, the way she did last Tuesday and the way she will again tomorrow.

02

Candor catches the client's details first

The name, the date of birth, the policy number. It hands back the same text with those blanked out, ready to paste. The words she typed are never uploaded or saved anywhere, by design.

03

The use gets written down

Which tool, which matter, who ran it, what for, and when. One line, added to a list that can be added to but never quietly changed.

04

An attorney reads the output and signs

Candor points out citations worth a second look and places where the AI hedged. It does not decide whether the work is right. The attorney does, and the attorney's signature is what carries weight.

05

Renewal comes around and your insurer asks

How does your firm use AI, and how do you supervise it. You print the answer instead of trying to remember it.

Who it is for

This is probably you if:

You run or manage a firm of roughly one to thirty attorneys.

Your staff use AI already, or you strongly suspect they do.

If someone asked today what was run last month and who checked it, you could not answer.

Your malpractice renewal is coming, and there are questions on the form that were not there before.

Candor is built to work in any practice area. We are starting with insurance and property, family law, estate planning, and general practice firms in South Florida.

Why bother now

Three groups started asking.

Malpractice insurers are adding AI questions at renewal. Bar regulators are publishing guidance on competence and supervision. Judges are issuing disclosure orders. All three want documentation that almost no firm keeps today, and none of it can be produced after the fact.
