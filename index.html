Most firms adopted AI faster than they adopted any rules about it. There is no log of what was run, no record of who reviewed it, and no way to show a carrier, a court, or a client that the work was supervised.

That gap is about to become a requirement. Malpractice carriers are beginning to ask about AI use at renewal. Bar regulators are writing guidance. Judges are issuing standing orders on disclosure. Every one of them will want documentation that almost no firm currently keeps.

Candor keeps it, without slowing anyone down.

What we're building

One connected record — from the moment AI is used to the report a carrier asks for.

  • Confidentiality pre-check BuiltScans text for client-identifying details before anyone pastes it into an outside AI tool, and hands back a redacted version.
  • AI use log BuiltA per-matter record of every AI use — tool, task, who, and when — measured against the firm's approved-tools list.
  • Attorney review & sign-off BuiltAn append-only record of attorney review. Corrections are new entries, never edits.
  • Carrier-ready reports BuiltA firm-wide summary and a carrier-renewal questionnaire, generated straight from the record.
  • Policy management PlannedAdopt a written AI policy and capture staff acknowledgements, with re-acknowledgement on updates.
  • Verification layer PlannedFlags citations and factual claims that may need a second look, before an attorney signs off.
