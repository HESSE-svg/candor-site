Security · Trust

The short version, for whoever signs off on vendors.

Ten facts about how Candor handles a firm’s data, a straight answer on certifications, and the documents behind them. The security page explains the design in depth.

What holds today

Encrypted, both ways

Data travels over TLS and is encrypted where it is stored.

The fact, not the content

Candor records that a screen ran and which kinds of details it flagged, never the screened text. Prompts and AI answers are not kept unless the firm turns on conversation history.

Nothing is rewritten

The record is append-only. A correction is a new entry that points to the original, and the chain of entries shows if anything was altered.

One firm, one record

Every record is scoped to the firm that owns it, so one firm’s entries never appear in another’s account.

Screening on the device

The confidentiality check runs in the browser. The text being screened is never uploaded.

Citations travel alone

To look up a case, only the bare citation string goes to CourtListener. Never the text around it, a client name or a matter name.

A click, not a watcher

The browser extension acts when someone asks it to. It never intercepts traffic, watches a session or records which sites people visit.

Walls from your own Clio

With matter walls on, each person sees only the matters their own Clio login can see. No connection means no matters.

Your firm’s AI account

Candor’s workspace runs on the model your firm connects, with its own key, stored encrypted and used only for the firm’s calls.

Hosted in the United States

The Candor software and its database run in the United States.

Certifications

Candor does not yet hold a SOC 2 report or an ISO 27001 certification. The first audit we plan is SOC 2 Type I; no date is set. Until then, we would rather say so plainly than imply otherwise, and we are glad to walk your IT or risk lead through the architecture.
